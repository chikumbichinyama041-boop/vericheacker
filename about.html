How it works

A plain explanation of what VeriChecker does, where its information comes from, and where it stops.

The check

  • You choose the institutionThe bank, mobile money provider, school or company the detail is supposed to belong to.
  • You enter what you were givenAn account number, phone number, paybill or merchant code, or an account holder name.
  • We compare it with the official recordYou see whether it matches, doesn’t match, or is almost the same as a real one.

Where the records come from

Every detail on VeriChecker is meant to come from the institution’s own official sources: its website, its official helpline, or its branch. We do not add details that were sent to us in a message or that only one person told us.

If we do not have a confirmed detail for an institution, the check says so instead of guessing.

Right now the records on this site are examples. We will say so clearly until real, confirmed records replace them.

“Looks like a fake”

Scammers often use a number that is one or two digits away from the real one, hoping nobody notices. When a detail is that close to an official one but not the same, VeriChecker shows both side by side and marks the digits that differ.

What VeriChecker cannot do

It can only tell you whether a detail is on an institution’s official list. It cannot tell you whether a request for money is genuine, and it cannot confirm that money has reached your account.

If a message or call you didn’t start asks you to pay, contact the institution yourself using a number from its official website or your card or statement.
